## Former design decision — retired

Build a **clear service website with the craft of a digital product studio**. The chosen homepage direction is [concept 03](homepage-concepts.md), keeping the small all-caps **“WHERE VISION TAKES FLIGHT”** above **“Build it. Market it. Automate it.”** and **“Branding, Marketing, Website development, content and AI automation.”** A few image apertures change rapidly with scroll through short fades while the words remain steady. The [UI design base](ui-design-base.md) is authoritative for this hero. Content production is the strongest existing visual proof. Web, SaaS, SEO, marketing, branding, and automation earn trust through precise offers, working demonstrations, deliverables, and process until their own case studies exist.

The current [Zavino homepage](https://thezavino.com/) already has 13 real reels, four still-image projects, confident type, dark evergreen and cream, and a flight-themed identity. It also leads with an evocative headline that leaves the expanded service offer unclear. Preserve the recognizable palette and the best real media; move the service answer and contact choices into view much sooner.

## Visual system proposal

### One brand, two kinds of proof

The design has to let cinematic media and technical service explanation coexist. Use a **two-speed layout**:

1. **Editorial frames** for claims and navigation: huge but readable Syne display type, small IBM Plex Sans labels, firm grid lines, open space, and one sentence that explains the offer. These fonts already exist in the current project.
2. **Evidence frames** for what people can inspect: real reels and stills for production/brand; interactive service diagrams, capability demonstrations, actual deliverable samples, and later verified case studies for web/marketing/automation. Illustrative demos must be labeled as examples or concepts.

Retain evergreen and warm cream as the base. Let one vivid accent identify actions and active states; do not turn every service into a separate color brand. The aviation theme can become a restrained navigation language—trajectory lines, coordinates, progress marks, or “from idea to launch”—while the offer copy stays literal. Use an asymmetric 12-column desktop grid, generous text margins, and full-width media only where the media itself explains a service. Each viewport should have one dominant visual idea, rather than a collage of equally loud cards.

### Archived hero: type-led promise, scroll-reactive proof

**Chosen sequence:**

1. Small tracked all-caps eyebrow: **“WHERE VISION TAKES FLIGHT”**, with its fine horizontal rule.
2. Main statement, in order: **“Build it. Market it. Automate it.”** Set as three expressive but readable beats.
3. Direct supporting line: **“Branding, Marketing, Website development, content and AI automation.”**
4. Actions: **Send a brief**, **Book an audit** at the supplied [Cal.com event](https://cal.com/zavino/audit), and **WhatsApp**. Keep them available while the hero media changes.
5. Proof surface: retain concept 03's wide and narrow image apertures. Begin with approved stills; as the visitor scrolls, swap through a short ordered sequence with fast crossfades. The words and controls stay stable. The interaction is specified in [UI design base](ui-design-base.md).
6. The five-pillar service index immediately follows the opening scene. Each row names the formal service area and links to its hub.

The current first-visit “Preparing for Takeoff” introduction is visually distinctive, but it delays the offer. In the redesign, any branded opening motion should complete quickly and never gate reading or clicking.

## Page-specific layout concepts

These are **design treatments**, not a competing URL map. Match them to the finalized page architecture and exact service wording.

### Homepage

- **First screen:** small all-caps brand eyebrow, approved headline and supporting line, three named contact paths, and two restrained media apertures that react to scroll.
- **Five-pillar navigator:** tall numbered rows; a hover/focus swaps a poster, diagram, or texture and shows one sentence about the output. On touch, the list is fully expanded and tappable.
- **Proof chapter:** 2–3 selected real production pieces shown as varied editorial compositions (one full-width film, one side-by-side image story, one compact reel row), not 13 thumbnails in a single wall. A link reaches the full collection.
- **Digital craft chapter:** a small interactive, clearly labeled **Zavino Lab** demo, such as a responsive mini storefront or a workflow explorer. It demonstrates interaction quality without pretending to be client work.
- **How engagement works:** three or four steps from audit/brief through scope, make, launch, and optimization. End with the three contact options.

### Services hub

- Begin with the five categories and an outcome-led one-liner for each. Give every category a distinct large-format layout: film frame, channel map, brand artifact grid, interactive product preview, automation flow.
- Include the complete service inventory in readable HTML, not only in animation or imagery. Where the list gets long, a sticky category index can shorten scanning on desktop.
- Show related service combinations as routes rather than bundles of unexplained jargon: for example, product film + Meta ads; ecommerce site + SEO; content generation + publishing workflow. These are example pairings, not package promises.
- Place a CTA after the overview and again after detailed sections. Copy should say **Discuss this service**, **Send a brief**, or **Book an audit**.

### Content Production

- Open with a large, paused real film frame and a one-line “what we make” statement.
- Use a horizontal editorial filmstrip for Cinematic Reels, Product Videos, Food Photography & Videography, Motion Graphics, and CGI Content. Each format reveals real relevant work if available. If a format has no approved example, show process/deliverables instead of filling with stock.
- A “from brief to frame” sequence can move through concept, production, finishing, and delivery. Film controls are explicit; video is loaded on play or by a carefully bounded viewport rule.
- A dedicated still-photo layout can use the [21st.dev image comparison](https://21st.dev/@kuratlielia/components/image-compare) idea for a **real** raw-to-final grade, crop, or packaging visualization. The reference demo has a draggable slider with a named control, which is stronger than an ornamental hover effect.

### Digital Marketing

- Use a crisp channel map: strategy at the center, Meta and Google channels around it, then measurement feeding back to decisions. A user selecting a channel changes the explanation panel; the text remains present in the document.
- Draw the campaign journey in annotated stages: goal → audience → creative → launch → tracking → iteration. A segmented control can switch between Meta and Google examples.
- Avoid fake dashboards, fabricated ROAS charts, and generic upward lines. If real anonymized reporting becomes available later, show its scope, date, and method.

### Branding & Creative

- Divide into three visual chapters: **identity systems**, **social/packaging and visual direction**, and **offline/event activation**. This respects the supplied hierarchy while keeping offline work visible.
- Use a variable-scale artifact grid instead of repeated cards: logo or type study, social template, packaging dieline, banner, and stall or activation layout. Each asset requires permission and a truthful label.
- An expandable “one idea across touchpoints” view can demonstrate consistency from screen to shelf to space. For missing work, render a labeled in-house concept exercise, not a client claim.

### Web Development / ecommerce / mini SaaS / custom web apps

- Give this page a **working interface as the hero evidence**. A small responsive prototype can change between phone and desktop and demonstrate a real user journey. Label it “Zavino Lab concept” until it comes from a real project.
- Three outcome-oriented offer panels explain Ecommerce Web Design (Shopify/WordPress), Custom Mini SaaS Development, and Custom Web App Development. Each panel should show who it is for, typical deliverables, integrations, client inputs, and what makes a project more complex.
- A scannable service comparison helps buyers choose between a commerce build and a custom application. Keep a direct brief CTA beside the comparison.
- Show a lifecycle/quality strip: discovery → UX/prototype → build → QA → launch → support. Use a responsive layout with a small amount of scroll-linked motion, never scroll lock.
- SEO deserves an explicit nested section or linked subpage. Show On-Page SEO, Technical SEO, Off-Page Link Building Strategy, Local SEO, and Citations as a layered system. The display should explain scope and outputs, not promise rankings.

### AI Automation

- Use a workflow canvas as the primary composition: input/trigger → n8n orchestration → asset generation (ComfyUI where relevant) → review/approval → publishing → monitoring. A line can animate once as the steps enter view; selecting a step reveals its purpose and deliverable.
- Separate Social Media Automation, AI Image & Video Generation, and Automated Content Publishing Workflow so visitors can ask for one without buying an undefined “AI solution.”
- Display a realistic example workflow **as an illustrative demo**, with no real client details or unverified savings figures. Make the human approval point visible where the proposed workflow needs it.

### Work, About, and Contact

- **Work:** start with the real production reel collection and existing still projects. Filters appear only for categories that actually contain items. Later, add case-study pages with a consistent metadata strip (client/permission, challenge, Zavino's role, deliverables, launch date, and measured outcome if verified). Include a separately labeled Lab area for self-initiated digital work.
- **About:** use a small number of real team photos, practices, and capabilities. Better to explain who does the work and how disciplines collaborate than to add unsourced award or client-count claims.
- **Contact:** use a visually quiet split surface: **WhatsApp for a quick question**, **Send a project brief**, **Book a 30-minute audit**. The form can follow NeuroX's short, structured pattern, with service and budget choices plus a free-text brief. It should state what happens next, and should never look like a page with only a decorative CTA.

## Motion and interaction budget

“Show off web development skill” should mean coherent behavior, not continuous animation. The interactions below each have a job:

| Interaction | Job | Proposed behavior | Reduced-motion / touch behavior |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Service index preview | Make five broad pillars quickly comparable | On hover or keyboard focus, change one adjacent image/diagram and highlight the active row. | All labels and summaries remain visible. On touch, tap navigates directly; no first-tap reveal trap. |
| Homepage hero image sequence | Show range and craft without burying the offer | Scroll progress changes approved stills in the two image apertures; each update uses a short opacity crossfade. Text and actions remain fixed in their reading order. | One representative still remains; the offer and actions are unchanged. On touch, ordinary page scrolling drives the sequence. |
| Process steps | Make delivery concrete | Step highlight follows scroll while an adjacent artifact/diagram changes. | Render a normal stacked sequence with every step visible. |
| Image comparison | Show a real transformation | Pointer-drag and keyboard-operable divider on approved before/after material. | Both labeled images can also be viewed separately. |
| Automation flow | Explain system connections | Short connecting-line reveal once; selection of a node changes explanatory text. | Static connectors; nodes remain buttons with visible focus and text alternatives. |
| CTA and menu | Confirm action | Crisp underline, color, and small translation feedback. | Focus state communicates the same affordance; no hover-only navigation. |

**Motion hierarchy:** the homepage's scroll-reactive image sequence is its one expressive scene. Other pages can use a filmstrip on Content or a workflow diagram on AI, with smaller transitions for state changes and otherwise calm reading surfaces. The hero sequence has no autoplay or continuous loop. If another continuously moving rail or marquee runs longer than five seconds beside other content, provide pause/stop/hide controls per [W3C's WCAG 2.2 guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide). Respect the operating-system [reduced-motion preference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/%40media/prefers-reduced-motion) with a complete static composition, rather than removing content. Prefer transforms and opacity for movement, and check actual frame rate; [MDN's animation performance guide](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Performance/Guides/Animation_performance_and_frame_rate) explains why layout-changing animation is more expensive.

Video and visual effects should not make the first useful content late. Load a sharp first hero still, then prepare nearby sequence frames as needed. Let users start any full reel themselves. Defer below-the-fold video and resource-heavy effects; avoid a global WebGL background, heavy cursor trail, or mandatory loader. Test desktop and mobile on real devices during implementation, especially scroll response, text readability over media, and tap targets in the service index.

## Portfolio-light launch strategy

The strongest version of this redesign is honest about what is already available:

1. **Publish now:** real Zavino production films and permitted stills, carefully captioned. Keep a work card tied to the actual service shown, rather than letting a content piece stand in for SaaS or SEO delivery.
2. **Explain now:** exact deliverables, engagement stages, choices, service boundaries, and who each offer helps. This is the trust content for services without work samples.
3. **Demonstrate now:** self-initiated interactive prototypes or example workflows with persistent “Concept / Zavino Lab” labeling. They can show front-end detail, responsive behavior, information design, and automation understanding.
4. **Add later:** verified case studies one by one. Add the outcome and testimonial fields only when permission and evidence exist. The layout should accept a new project without redesigning the page.

This also gives the site a content cadence: new reel → content page and work archive; new ecommerce launch → web page and case study; new automation project → automation page and explainer; new learning article → relevant service page. Until those arrive, empty categories and “coming soon” tiles should be avoided.

## Priority calls for the design phase

1. Apply the approved hero copy and first-screen hierarchy from [UI design base](ui-design-base.md). A stranger should understand the service range and three contact routes without scrolling.
2. Inventory every approved reel/still and map each to a service claim. Curate the short homepage scroll sequence and choose its initial frames.
3. Design one shared visual system, then give each pillar one distinct signature layout. Do not use the same card grid five times.
4. Prototype the Web and AI interaction modules as labeled Zavino Lab pieces. They should be usable demonstrations, not decorative screenshots.
5. Build motion after the static mobile/desktop layouts read well. Verify keyboard behavior, reduced motion, media controls, and performance before calling the design complete.
