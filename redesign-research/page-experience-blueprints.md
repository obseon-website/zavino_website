# Page experience blueprints — AI-first site

**Proposed future experiences, 4 October 2026.** The [current direction](current-direction.md) sets the message; the [page map](service-architecture-and-page-map.md) sets route priority; the [motion brief](motion-and-interaction-direction.md) sets interaction behavior. These sections are recommendations for a later builder, not pages already implemented.

## Shared design language

Use Zavino's dark evergreen as a recognizable base, sharpened toward charcoal, warm ivory, and a single controlled luminous accent. Typography should be expressive and highly readable. Layouts should feel authored: generous negative space, variable section scale, deliberate asymmetry, precise diagram and interface details. Give each primary offer its own visual form rather than using the same card grid three times.

The site should **show excellent frontend work**: responsive compositions that change intelligently, semantic and keyboard-operable interactions, smooth localized CSS motion, fast first content, and a polished static state. The biggest spectacle should be the system illustration that helps explain automation. A buyer must understand the offer even if every animation is disabled.

## Services index

Begin with the three primary offers, each with a different visual artifact: a governed workflow for AI Automation, a product interface for SaaS, and a responsive site/platform for Web. Use one short buyer question per offer and a link to its own hub. After the primary selection, show “Additional capabilities” with honest summaries of AI UGC, custom AI, content production, branding, digital marketing, SEO, and offline work. Keep full service text crawlable and tappable. A desktop preview may respond to focus or hover, but mobile visitors should never need a first tap merely to reveal a link.

## AI Automation hub

**Opening claim:** “Connect the systems, decisions, and people behind the work.” Explain the fit: complex, repeatable workflows with meaningful volume or impact. Present three example problem families: customer lifecycle/offer orchestration, service/support operations, and internal document or approval workflows. They are **example designs**, not client results.

**Core visual:** a transparent system canvas. A visitor can inspect trigger, data source, eligibility/rules, AI task, human review, action, logging, and failure recovery. A scenario switch can change the surface example while leaving the architecture comprehensible. One state should visibly pause at an approval gate. Give a plain-text explanation beside the diagram.

**Content sequence:** buyer problem → what Zavino maps first → architecture and integration boundaries → guardrails and human ownership → deliverables → rollout and measurement → FAQ → contextual brief. Explain when AI is actually needed and when deterministic logic is safer. Discuss consent, permissions, auditability, and escalation without making unsupported compliance claims. CTA: **Map an automation project**.

## SaaS Development hub

Lead with product ambition and operating reality: from a new concept or fragile prototype to a product people can use and pay for. A high-quality, labeled concept UI should demonstrate **customer view, admin workflow, and an operational state**, rather than fake revenue graphs. On mobile it should become a readable sequence, not a reduced desktop screenshot.

Cover product discovery, MVP scope, UX, frontend/backend architecture, data and user roles, multi-tenancy where relevant, billing if needed, integrations, testing, deployment, instrumentation, and iteration. Show ownership and handover as questions to answer in scope, not blanket promises. Include a “first release vs later releases” decision framework so large ambition still has a buildable start. CTA: **Discuss a SaaS build**.

## Web Development hub

Make the breadth of web work clear without turning the page into an undifferentiated checklist. Start with four paths: **business websites, ecommerce, portals/internal tools, and custom web applications**. A small responsive demo can switch between desktop and mobile views of the same experience. Label in-house demos accurately.

Show a buyer how Zavino handles UX, content/CMS, integrations, accessibility, speed, frontend/backend quality, analytics, technical SEO, QA, launch, and ongoing improvement. Explain Shopify/WordPress fit as a discussion, not a universal answer. Link to SEO and growth work where it helps the buyer. A separate SEO route should wait until it can say more than this hub. CTA: **Discuss a web platform**.

## Supporting capabilities

- **AI UGC and custom AI:** show creative concepts or workflow samples with clear disclosure of synthetic media, rights, approval, and brand review. Explain where these fit into campaign and lifecycle systems. Do not use false customer testimonials or imply a generated character is a real client.
- **Content Production:** use approved films and stills, captions stating Zavino's actual role, and poster-first click-to-play media. Let the work carry the visual weight.
- **Digital Marketing:** show how strategy, creative, Meta/Google execution, tracking, and iteration connect. No invented ROAS charts or guaranteed returns.
- **Branding & Creative / Offline:** use real identity and physical artifacts where authorized. Explain the relationship between system, touchpoints, and activation. Do not fill missing categories with misattributed work.
- **SEO:** cover on-page, technical, off-page strategy, local, and citations with exact deliverables and no ranking promise.

These areas can start as substantial sections within Services. Publish separate routes only when they have distinct copy, proof or process, and a relevant CTA.

## Work, About, and Contact

**Work:** split **verified commissioned work** from **Zavino concepts**. Project cards should say status, sector, Zavino's actual role, deliverables, and outcome only when verified. Existing reels can be a strong creative archive while AI and SaaS proof grows. Never put a creative film under an automation case-study label.

**About:** explain who does the work, where the team operates, how scoping and delivery are managed, and how design, engineering, AI, and creative disciplines connect. Use real people and process material where available. Avoid generic “trusted by global brands” claims without evidence.

**Contact:** make a serious-project brief primary and direct scheduling easy. The [conversion plan](conversion-funnel-and-lead-form.md) gives the fields, context, delivery states, and qualification questions. Keep WhatsApp and email as genuine shortcuts. Form feedback should be immediate, readable, and stable.
